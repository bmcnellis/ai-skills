# Exemplars

Every sentence below is quoted verbatim from McNellis et al. (2021, *Ecosphere*) or McNellis et al. (2023, *Environ. Res. Lett.*). They are the evidence behind the rules in `profile.md`; when a rule and an exemplar seem to disagree, the exemplar wins.

## Contrast lives inside the sentence

`but` appears 28 times and never once opens a sentence; `while` opens 13 of its 24 uses. Contrast is a hinge in the middle of a clause, not a connective bolted to the front.

> Class imbalance sampling was not stratified by tree size or species, but diameter and species distributions in the final training dataset were very similar to the overall dataset.

> Fire was also excluded from this study due to its stochastic nature and localized area of effect, but much previous work has identified fire as a major driver of tree mortality and ecosystem change in the Sierra Nevada, Klamath Mountains, and other parts of California.

> While research in other systems has examined the relationship between plant cover and grazing in the context of landscape setting, few studies have attempted to examine their joint effects on a landscape as heterogeneous as the Colorado Plateau.


## Concession uses `Nonetheless`, not `However`

`Nonetheless` opens 6 sentences; `However` opens 3 in the whole corpus. The move is: state the limitation, then reassert what still holds.

> Nonetheless, tree size alone was not enough to account for observed mortality rates and was unlikely to be responsible for increases in mortality in the future (Uriarte et al. 2004, Zhang et al. 2013, Birch et al. 2015).

> Nonetheless, our analysis here necessarily assumes that livestock removal was started and finished before the first year without grazing.


## Discussion paragraphs close on a forward-looking recommendation

10 of 23 Discussion paragraphs end this way, and 13 of 23 end on a hedge. The closer names who should do what next, and often what it would buy.

> Future work utilizing FIA data would benefit from a more robust understanding of the limitations imposed by FIA mortality agent methodology, including limits posed by on-the-ground accuracy that are independent of field technician training.

> Future work on tree mortality would benefit from a better understanding of how fire interacts with other mortality agents as well as finer resolution of the relationship between local fire severity and individual tree death.

> Improved measures of livestock movement across the landscape (e.g. GPS collars; Bailey et al 2021) may improve estimates of grazing intensity in future studies.


## Interpretation is hedged with `may`, and often twice in one sentence

`may` occurs 59 times: 56 of them in the Discussion (14.4 per 1,000 words), none at all in the Results. 20 sentences carry two or more hedges.

> Conversely, long-lived tree species such as P. menzeisii or members of Juniperus may live more than 1000 yr and may not die for some time after the onset of lethal environmental or biotic stress (Alexander et al. 2018).

> Our results somewhat agree with previous work that suggests future forest mortality may be a widespread phenomenon, but we emphasize that some species and regions may be disproportionately affected (Allen et al. 2010, Kane et al. 2014, McDowell and Allen 2015).

> Combined with our results here, this may indicate that Rocky Mountain forests may be disproportionately affected by drier, warmer temperatures, even when compared to other regions in the western USA.


## Results are impersonal, uncited, and carry the numbers in parentheses

No Results sentence contains a citation, and only one contains a first-person pronoun. 45% put a value in parentheses and 29% reference a figure or table.

> All three significant intercepts fit to CDW were in Cathedral: bare soil cover in 'Very High' (−14.59 ± 6.79), perennial herbaceous cover in 'Very Low' (2.41 ± 1.45), and total foliar cover in 'Very High' (9.18 ± 3.66).

> Varying slopes fit to fSCA for each level of CDW were significant for 20 out of 24 levels (figure 4).

> Mortality model prediction accuracy (PCC) during training was 71.3% with class error rates for alive and dead trees of 27.8% and 29.5%, respectively.


## Methods put the thing measured in subject position

43% of Methods sentences carry a passive construction, against 19% in the Discussion. The agent is omitted because the procedure, not the operator, is the point.

> Climate variables were calculated for each plot based on the mean value for the 10 yr previous to the re-measurement inventory year.

> Mann-Whitney U-tests were performed on pairs of provinces and sections, as well as between predicted and recent mortality rates, to test for statistically significant differences (Hollander and Wolfe 1973).

> Bare soil and perennial herbaceous cover were sourced from the Rangeland Analysis Platform version 3 in November 2021 (Allred et al 2021).


## `we` marks a decision, not an action

First person appears in 28% of Methods sentences but is attached to choices a reader could have made differently -- what was included, excluded, defined, assumed.

> We limited consideration to the 53 most common tree species due to restrictions imposed by our analysis software.

> We utilize a multi-model approach to evaluate progressively more complex hypotheses while guarding against overfitting.

> We modeled the effect of livestock removal on landscape cover as the number of years since counterfactual intervention ('YSI').


## Terms are defined in single quotes at first use and then used bare

11 sentences introduce a term or abbreviation this way, inside the sentence that first needs it, rather than in a glossary or a definition sentence of its own.

> Livestock grazing is a dominant land use in semi-arid and arid landscapes ('drylands' hereafter; Maestre et al 2016) where dry conditions preclude other uses such as crop cultivation (Thornton et al 2009, Hoover et al 2020).

> Climate variables sourced from Daymet version 4 monthly climate summaries included the sum of monthly precipitation ('PPT'), monthly mean maximum temperature ('Tmax', ◦C), and monthly mean minimum temperature ('Tmin', ◦C; Thornton et al 2020).


## Citations are parenthetical, bundled, and chronological

36% of sentences carry a parenthetical citation and only 1.8% name an author as the subject. 40% of parentheses hold more than one source, up to seven.

> Tree mortality is one of the most sudden and significant demographic processes within forested ecosystems (Waring 1987, Allen et al. 2010, Kosiba et al. 2018).

> Previous work on forest mortality has largely been done in a small number of well-studied model systems (Allen and Breshears 1998, Geils et al. 2010, Hicke and Zeppel 2013).

> Tinkham et al. (2018) provide a thorough overview of FIA data, sampling procedures, and applications.


## Paragraphs open on a noun phrase naming the topic

54 of 73 paragraphs open this way; exactly one opens with a connective. The first sentence states the claim or the object of the paragraph, and the citations follow it.

> Tree diameter was the 3rd most important variable for improving model accuracy and contributed significantly to predicting alive trees.

> Soil systems of the Colorado Plateau are highly complex due to the interactive effects of geology, geomorphology, and pedogenesis (Duniway et al 2022).

> Understanding landscape resilience to livestock grazing intensity is a central tenet of rangeland science.
